## China: Record loan contraction, limited govt bond pickup

2Q Monetary Policy Report leaned a bit more dovish

- New  lending  remains  the  key  disappointment  with  a  record  contraction, reinforcing lingering domestic demand weakness.
- Non-loan  TSF  provided  a  partial  cushion,  supported  by  stronger  bond financing, a milder shadow-credit drag, and a pickup in equity financing.
- Loan growth fell to a fresh low of 5.1%oya, while TSF stabilized at 7.4%.
- PBOC struck a slightly more dovish tone in 2Q monetary policy report; we continue to see a 10bp rate cut in 4Q as growth and demand remain soft.
- Reform towards overnight-rate-centered framework remains the near-term focus.

The main disappointment in July's credit report came from new lending again, which recorded a record net contraction amid pervasive weakness across sectors and loan tenors. Broad-based loan demand weakness pushed loan growth to a fresh low of 5.1%oya. Non-loan TSF flows modestly outperformed expectations, supported by decent bond issuance, a smaller-than-expected contraction in shadow credit, and a pickup in equity financing amid the IPO of major memory chip producer CXMT.

New loan creation (on a net basis) contracted by 340bn yuan (vs. BBG consensus: -100bn; JPMe: +81bn), marking the largest decline on record. Seasonally adjusted trend loan growth slowed further to 4.6%3m/3m saar, the weakest pace since mid2005. Weakness was broad-based, with most loan categories recording contractions. Medium- to long-term loans fell 350bn yuan, including -120bn for households and -230bn for corporates, reinforcing the picture of weak domestic demand seen in the softer-than-expected July inflation  data.  Corporate  short-term  loans  and  bill  financing provided a partial offset, rising 125.7bn yuan, as banks continued to rely on shortduration credit to meet lending targets amid weak medium- to long-term demand.

Overall loan growth slowed to a fresh record low of 5.1%oya, while bank loan growth excluding interest payments decelerated further to 2.0%oya. Reflecting the sharp slowdown in lending, the share of outstanding RMB loans in total TSF fell to 60.1% at  end-July,  down 1.3%pts from a year earlier. At the July press conference, the PBOC framed weak loan growth as a consequence of China's structural transition away from credit-intensive property and infrastructure investment. While we cut our year-end loan growth forecast by 0.3%pt to 5.0%oya last month, a stabilization in lending will likely hinge on the deployment of the policy bank tool, which is a key element in the July Politburo's push to mobilize the remaining fiscal capacity approved at the March NPC.

TSF flows exceeded market expectations  at  1406bn  yuan,  though  closer  to  our forecast  (vs.  consensus:  1081bn;  JPMe:  1274bn),  driven  by  stronger  non-loan financing. Government bond financing came in above our alt-data tracking at 1320bn yuan, likely reflecting the inclusion of roughly 200bn yuan of June-end issuance not captured in last month's data. Taken together, June-July government bond financing averaged 1044bn yuan, broadly in line with our tracking. Corporate bond issuance improved further to  452bn  yuan,  while  shadow  credit  remained  in  contraction  at  -78bn yuan, albeit less negative than expected. ## Enhance rate operational mechanism; a mild dovish tilt

The PBOC released its 2Q Monetary Policy Implementation Report this week, which struck a modestly  more  dovish  tone  than  the  1Q  report  and  was  broadly  consistent  with  the  July Politburo's  policy  guidance.  While  the  1Q  report  emphasized  the  'precise  and  effective implementation' of an appropriately accommodative stance, the 2Q report introduced stronger language around 'rolling out practical incremental policies in a timely manner', 'stepping up countercyclical  adjustment',  and  'strengthening  domestic  demand'.  Key  takeaways  are summarized below:

Further  refine  short-term  rate  operational  framework .  The  PBOC  is  accelerating  its transition toward an overnight-rate-centered operating framework, bringing it closer to global central bank practice. Recent reforms, including a narrower ±25bp overnight rate corridor, rulesbased interventions tied to DR001 deviations, and the introduction of overnight reverse repos, all strengthen the role of overnight rates in policy implementation. With over 90% of interbank repo  transactions  now  conducted  overnight,  the  growing  use  of  overnight  OMOs  should  improve liquidity management and policy transmission. The PBOC committed to increase the use of overnight reverse repos, aiming to make DR001 the primary target rate, 7-day reverse repo rate the key policy rate, and overnight OMOs the main operating tools in China's evolving monetary policy framework.

Reduced emphasis on price reflation .  The 1Q report repeatedly highlighted the target of promoting a 'reasonable recovery in prices', and linking it explicitly to monetary policy. In contrast, the 2Q report dropped much of this language and instead emphasized aligning credit and money growth with economic growth and inflation targets. This suggests that while low inflation remains a concern, policymakers are placing relatively greater weight on stabilizing growth and demand than on achieving a near-term rebound in prices.

More cautious assessment of external risks .  The 2Q report adopts a more cautious tone on the external backdrop, upgrading it from 'deepening external changes' to a 'more complex and volatile  environment,'  while  highlighting  more  frequent  geopolitical  conflicts  and  trade frictions. This suggests heightened concern over external uncertainties and potential spillovers from global geopolitical and trade tensions.

No sign of increased concern over RMB appreciation . There was no major narrative change around the FX exchange rate. The report highlighted the RMB's 3.0% appreciation against the USD and 4.7% gain on a CFETS basis in 1H, while refraining from introducing stronger rhetoric on managing appreciation pressures. Against the backdrop of heightened debate over global imbalances and currency undervaluation, as well as the recent US-Japan FX intervention, the PBOC appears comfortable with greater FX flexibility and does not signal rising concern over RMB strength at this stage.

Taken together, consistent with the July Politburo's emphasis on fiscal implementation as nearterm priority, the PBOC appears focused on improving policy transmission and delivering targeted support in the near term, while remaining open to further easing should growth and domestic demand stay soft. We think a rate cut before year-end remains on the table and have a 10bp rate cut in 4Q in our baseline forecast.
