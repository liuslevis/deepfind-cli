## From Weak to Weaker

## A Weak Batch of Data

China's July activity data weakened from June and disappointed already-low market expectations. Industrial production (IP) was relatively resilient, rising 4.5% yoy, supported by robust export growth. By contrast, retail sales grew only 0.6% yoy, and fixed asset investment (FAI) contracted 6.7% yoy in the first seven months of this year, implying double-digit year-over-year declines in July. With the construction sector still contracting and high-frequency data not pointing to a noticeable pickup, the July official IP and services output index suggest that China's official real GDP growth is running at a pace of around 4% yoy in the first half of Q3. 1

Following the weaker-than-expected macro data and the PBOC's mid-month overnight reverse repo operations to inject liquidity, our conversations with traders and investors suggest that market expectations of monetary policy easing increased somewhat. The yield on 10-year Chinese Government Bonds (CGB) also fell below 1.70%. Compared with end-February, just before the start of the Iran War, 10-year CGB yields have declined by more than 10bp, suggesting the market's view that this year's energy-driven inflation is likely temporary, growth headwinds remain strong, and policy easing is warranted. In sharp contrast, yields on 10-year government bonds in other countries have climbed substantially since late February (Exhibit 2).

## Why We Are More Concerned About July Data Weakness

Over the past year, we have observed a consistent pattern of IP and the NBS manufacturing PMI strengthening into quarter-end months, such as March, June, and September, before falling sequentially in subsequent months, such as April, July, and October (Exhibit 5). In our view, this pattern may reflect manufacturing output being frontloaded into quarter-end months to support stronger readings of production-based official GDP measures. Back in April, we were not too concerned about the sequential decline in growth momentum. However, we think the July weakness points to increased downside risks for three reasons.

First, the April growth deceleration came after 5.0% yoy real GDP growth in Q1, which was at the upper end of this year's 4.5-5% growth target range. By contrast, the July growth deceleration followed 4.3% yoy real GDP growth in Q2, which was below the lower bound of the range.

Second, some sectors that had appeared organically strong showed fading momentum in July. For example, within retail sales, services sales growth had been running above 5% yoy earlier this year, significantly outperforming goods sales. In July, however, services sales growth dropped to 3.2% yoy by our estimates, raising questions about the outlook for household consumption, as goods sales are expected to remain soft amid the fading boost from the consumer goods trade-in program (Exhibit 6).

## Causes of the Persistent Weakness

One fundamental reason why the domestic economy has softened again, despite the easing package announced in September 2024 and the RMB10tn debt resolution plan approved in November 2024, is that policy support over the past few years has been too small relative to the size of the shock. One example is the evolution of the debt-service ratio (DSR) of the private non-financial sector. According to BIS data, the US private non-financial sector DSR peaked in 2007Q4 at the start of the subprime crisis (Exhibit 8). Significant Fed rate cuts and various government debt restructuring programs helped reduce the DSR by more than 4 percentage points over the ensuing seven years.

<!-- page: 5 -->

In contrast, China's private non-financial sector DSR initially peaked in 2020Q3, when the 'three red lines' policy aimed at deleveraging the property market was first introduced. But later, between 2022Q1 and 2025Q4, China's DSR increased by almost 2 percentage points. In other words, against the backdrop of three years of Covid lockdowns and a severe housing downturn, China's private sector had to allocate a rising share of income to debt servicing over the past five years. These elevated financial burdens squeezed purchasing power, weighed on sentiment, and left the domestic economy prone to notable weakening whenever growth headwinds emerged.

Another fundamental reason lies in the government's strategy of focusing almost exclusively on tech innovation and high-tech manufacturing. Before the Covid pandemic, the employment subindex of the NBS non-manufacturing PMI significantly outperformed that of the NBS manufacturing PMI. Post-Covid, however, this relationship completely reversed (Exhibit 9). While manufacturing employment appears better supported in recent years, it accounts for only around 20% of China's total employment and is insufficient to offset weakness in non-manufacturing employment. Put differently, while the policy emphasis on tech innovation and high-tech manufacturing has led to breakthroughs such as DeepSeek, as well as increased manufacturing competitiveness and persistent export strength, it has not translated into broad-based employment and income growth, which are crucial to a sustained increase in domestic demand.

## Policy Plans in Pipeline and Their Limits

To be clear, despite the weak July activity data and the downside risk to our Q3 GDP forecast, we still believe the government will manage to achieve at least 4.5% official real GDP growth this year and meet its target, barring extraordinary external shocks. After all, there have been only two instances when the government missed its growth target:

407b807cc90e11dcbf7a0014c23fbb6a once in 1998 during the Asian Financial Crisis, and again in 2022, when the spread of the Omicron variant proved too difficult to contain. The July Politburo meeting already turned more dovish by calling for ' strengthening countercyclical measures .' Various official announcements and government meetings after the July Politburo meeting point to four types of policies in the remainder of the year to support GDP growth (Exhibit 10).

<!-- page: 6 -->

- Investment : As of mid-August, nearly half of the full-year government bond n issuance quota remained untapped. To our knowledge, the RMB800bn New Policy-Based Financing Instrument (NPBFI) allocated for 2026 had not been used either. The first step would therefore be to accelerate government bond issuance and kick-start the NPBFI program. Targeted areas of spending are likely to include the 'Six Networks' (i.e., water networks, new power grids, computing power networks, next-generation communication networks, urban underground pipeline networks, and logistics networks) and the major projects outlined in the 15th Five-Year Plan.
- Industry : During the 12th State Council plenary session on August 17, Premier Li n Qiang called for a focus on ' sectors characterized by long value chains and large scale ' to boost domestic demand. Although property, autos, home appliances, electronic equipment and machinery all fit this description, the first three appear unlikely to see major new policies stimulating production, as property destocking is still ongoing and auto and home appliance sales were already frontloaded by the government-subsidized consumer goods trade-in program. This leaves electronic equipment and machinery, including semiconductors, as the likely sectors to receive additional policy support to expand production.
- Housing : On August 18, the State Council announced changes to the Housing n Provident Fund (HPF) rules to expand its use. The HPF is a mandatory, government-backed savings scheme in which employers and employees both contribute to individual accounts for housing-related consumption, such as buying, renting, or renovating a home. Currently, interest rates on HPF loans are 50bp lower than mortgage rates for first-time homebuyers. However, we think the impact of the latest announcement will only have a marginal impact on the property market, as many cities have already relaxed local HPF rules, benefiting low-end properties.
- Consumption : On August 13, the Ministry of Commerce, along with eight other n ministries, released a document on stimulating consumption in lower-tier cities and rural areas. Policymakers regard these areas as having greater demand for consumption upgrading. However, many of the measures focus on the supply side, including renovating commercial facilities and expanding the supply of high-quality, premium-priced goods. Without bolstering income, overall consumption is unlikely to rise simply because of increased supply of goods and services.

It remains to be seen how effective these policies will be, and whether policymakers will need to roll out additional easing measures in October (after seeing Q3 real GDP growth) in order to ensure the full-year growth target is met. In recent months, the government has introduced several safety- and security-related measures, including a special campaign against organized crime and tightened border control regulations. While safety and security need to be protected, these policies, especially when aggressively implemented, could have a negative impact on economic activity. For example, after the Shanxi coal mine accident in May, Chinese coal production dropped 12%, similar to the decline in January-February 2020 when the country was under a national Covid lockdown (Exhibit 11). In the current environment, where top leadership and local officials place strong emphasis on safety and security, the transmission from policy easing to economic activity may be blunted.

Even if the cyclical measures listed in Exhibit 10 help the government achieve the GDP growth target this year, they are unlikely to generate sustained growth momentum. This package of measures appears mostly supply-driven, raising output and production-based GDP measures in the near term but often resulting in oversupply, deflation, and increased debt over time. Another risk from the continued lack of demand-side stimulus and fundamental reforms to unleash domestic demand is that cyclical weakness could turn into structural hysteresis. As discussed earlier, we believe the magnitude of policy easing over the past few years has been too small relative to the size of the shock, and the cautious, intermittent style of policy response appears to persist. If continued for a few more years, prolonged financial stress on households, private businesses, and local governments could impair future growth potential.
