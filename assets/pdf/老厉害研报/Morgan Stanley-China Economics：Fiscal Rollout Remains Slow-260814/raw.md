## China Economics  |  Asia Pacific

## Fiscal Rollout Remains Slow

## Key Takeaways

- Broad credit growth YoY   edged up 10bps, to 7.6% (consensus: 7.5%), aided by stronger corporate bond issuance, but overall credit demand remained subdued. ■
- Continued slow public financing: unused fiscal and quasi-fiscal budget remains Rmb2trn more than last year as of end-July; no sign of funding acceleration MTD. ■
- Private credit demand remains weak: household deleveraging continues; improved corporate bond financing   failed to offset weakening longer-term corporate loans. ■
- Outlook: slow July-August public financing brings downside risk to our 4.5%Y 3Q GDP growth tracking; a big miss would make fresh stimulus this fall more likely. ■

Public financing remains slow: YoY government   bond issuance was flat in July, leaving Rmb6.1trn of the annual quota (44%) untapped-well above 2025's Rmb4.4trn. Combined with the Rmb800bn of unused policy-based instruments (Rmb500bn a year earlier), this keeps the in-budget fiscal impulse elevated at Rmb2trn. Yet MTD issuance ran markedly slower than last year, defying the Politburo's push for faster rollout -likely reflecting tighter local project scrutiny and a still-thin "six networks" pipeline.

Rising downside risk to 3Q GDP, September-October a catalyst window: Belowtarget 2Q GDP growth (4.3%) confirmed that the upper part of the K-shaped economy (exports, high-tech) can't yet offset weakness in the lower part (property, consumption), leaving fiscal impulse the key near-term swing factor. Slow government   bond issuance in July-August suggests that infrastructure capex may not accelerate till late September/early 4Q, posing downside risk to our 4.5%Y 3Q GDP growth tracking. Should data keep disappointing through August-September, the chance of additional easing in September-October would rise.
